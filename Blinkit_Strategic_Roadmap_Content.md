## Page 01/08 — Executive Summary: The State of the Quick-Commerce Union

Blinkit enters FY27 no longer proving the quick-commerce model works — it's proving what a profitable, India-scale version of it looks like.

Blinkit holds an estimated **46–50% share** of India's quick-commerce order value, roughly double its nearest rival, on a network that crossed **2,443 dark stores** at the end of Q1 FY27 — up 200 net-new stores in a single quarter — with a public line of sight to 3,000 by March 2027. The business has completed its transition from a commission marketplace to a high-margin, inventory-led model, with the large majority of Net Order Value now fulfilled through owned inventory rather than third-party listings.

What changed this year is the P&L. Net Order Value grew **86% year-on-year to ₹17,132 crore** in the quarter ended June 2026, and the quick-commerce segment posted a fifth consecutive quarter of adjusted-EBITDA improvement. Parent Eternal's own center of gravity has shifted with it: Blinkit is now the group's largest revenue engine, and in February 2026 co-founder Albinder Dhindsa was elevated to Group CEO of Eternal — the clearest possible signal that quick commerce, not food delivery, now sets the company's strategic agenda.

Profitability at this stage is an inflection, not a destination. Store-level economics still carry real fragility — a ₹2.5 crore capex load per store, a 12-day working-capital cycle, and a company-guided 40% steady-state ROCE target that has not yet been proven at scale outside dense metro clusters. The next phase of growth has to be earned in three places at once: extracting more margin from advertising without degrading the shopping experience, making Tier-2/3 expansion actually pay for itself, and keeping the human and regulatory foundation of the business — riders, kiranas, and city governments — from becoming a brake on growth.

This roadmap treats those three problems as one strategy, not three initiatives. It proposes a shift in how Blinkit talks about itself internally: from a **delivery app measured on minutes**, to a **unified commerce operating system measured on trust, density, and margin per order**.

> **Thesis:** The 10-minute promise got Blinkit to leadership. It won't be what defends it — reliability, assortment depth, and monetization precision will.

> **Scope of this document:** Eight sections covering monetization, Tier-2/3 economics, AI-driven operations, social sustainability, prioritization (RICE), and a quarterly execution roadmap.

## Page 03/08 — Market Positioning & Competitive Landscape

Six players, one genuinely defensible lead — but the fight is bifurcating into a metro-density race and a Tier-2/3 reach race at the same time.

The quick-commerce field has consolidated to six real players. Blinkit holds an estimated 46–50% of order value, with Swiggy Instamart (~24%) and Zepto (~22%) as the only competitors with comparable dark-store density. Amazon Now and Flipkart Minutes have both scaled past 500 dark stores in under two years, and BigBasket's BB Now trades share for reach, riding Tata's sourcing network into cities the pure-plays haven't reached yet.

Positioning both by **inventory model** (owned vs. marketplace) and **geographic depth** (metro vs. Tier-2/3) shows why Blinkit's lead is real but not permanent: it's the only player scaled in the top-right "owned inventory + Tier-2/3 reach" quadrant, but Instamart and Zepto are both dense enough to contest metros directly, and the hybrids are the ones actually testing Tier-2/3 economics from a different angle — lower capex, lower control.

> **Signal to watch:** Albinder Dhindsa's move to Group CEO of Eternal in Feb 2026 — with Deepinder Goyal shifting to non-executive Vice Chairman — makes Blinkit's operating logic the operating logic for the whole company. Expect capital and org priority to keep flowing here first.

## Page 04/08 — Pillar 01: Monetization Precision — Beyond Delivery Fees: Brand Central 2.0

Ad revenue is already growing faster than GMV. The job now is precision — shifting the objective from "Top of Mind" to "Top of Cart."

### Initiative 1: Predictive Bidding Engine

Search behavior is sharply time-boxed — breakfast-adjacent queries peak 6–9am, snacks 5–7pm. A session-based, dayparted bidding layer lets brands pay a premium specifically for these "Intent Windows" instead of flat all-day placements, which should lift realized ROAS without raising the total ad load a shopper sees.

### Initiative 2: Sponsored Sampling

Brands spend heavily on offline trial — sachets, in-store demos, sampling drives — with no attribution. Blinkit can convert that budget into a measurable digital format: **Sponsored Sampling** lets brands pay to insert sample-size SKUs into high-value carts (₹500+ orders). The shopper gets a free trial; the brand gets a tracked conversion path from sample → full-size purchase within 30 days. This turns Blinkit's delivery network into a last-mile sampling channel — a media unit no competitor currently offers.

Alongside Sponsored Sampling, upgrade the **Search Keyword Bidding Engine** to support category-level keyword auctions (e.g., "protein bar," "face wash") where brands bid on search terms the way they bid on Google — cost-per-click, real-time auction, quality-score weighted. This is the natural evolution of the existing ad stack, not a new platform.

### Initiative 3: Bundle Bidding

Curated "Solution Bundles" (a Sunday Brunch bundle of milk, bread, and jam) let brands bid to be the pre-selected default within a bundle slot — a higher-intent, higher-CPM unit than a standalone search ad, borrowed from bundle-monetization patterns that already work in other curated marketplaces.

> **Why this pillar first:** Ad revenue grew 220% YoY — nearly 3x GMV growth. That gap is the single highest-margin lever on this entire roadmap, and none of it requires new capex.

## Page 05/08 — Pillar 02: Structural Reach — Solving the "Bharat" Problem

Lower rent and lower labor costs cut the breakeven bar in smaller cities — but lower basket sizes can eat the saving entirely if the product doesn't adapt.

### Initiative 1: Minimum Order Value (MOV) Tiers

Tier-2/3 expansion is a genuine paradox. A dark store in a smaller city needs roughly **800 orders/day** to break even, versus **1,300 in a metro** — real estate and labor cost less. But survey data consistently shows these users shopping for deals and value first, speed second, which means the average order looks nothing like a metro basket, and the store can hit its order count without hitting its revenue target.

The fix isn't to slow down delivery — speed is Blinkit's moat, and surrendering it in any market hands competitors the opening they need. The fix is to **raise average basket value through dynamic MOV Tiers**: city-level minimum order thresholds that unlock free delivery, paired with product-level interventions that make hitting the threshold feel like a deal, not a penalty.

Three levers work together:
- **Dynamic basket thresholds:** City-specific MOV (e.g., ₹199 in Tier-2, ₹149 in Tier-3) calibrated weekly against store-level contribution margin. An "Add ₹X more for free delivery" nudge converts 20–30% of sub-threshold carts in comparable models.
- **Multi-pack bundles:** Auto-generated "Buy 2, Save 15%" offers on high-frequency staples (oil, atta, dal) placed at cart review — drives AOV without new SKU complexity.
- **Value-focused private label placement:** Blinkit's private-label SKUs carry 2–3× the gross margin of branded equivalents. Tier-2/3 homepages should lead with private-label shelf space in staples, not national brands — the margin math is structurally different from metros.

### Initiative 2: High-Margin Assortment & Reverse Logistics

The next AOV unlock isn't more grocery SKUs — it's **high-margin categories that dark stores are uniquely positioned to serve**: electronics accessories (₹800+ AOV, 40%+ margin), beauty and personal care (₹500+ AOV, 35%+ margin), and select apparel (athleisure, innerwear — ₹600+ AOV, 45%+ margin).

The barrier to these categories online has always been returns friction. A dark store within 2–3 km of the customer can solve this: **10-minute reverse logistics**. Customer initiates a return in-app, a rider is dispatched to pick up the item, and if it's an exchange, the replacement is delivered simultaneously from the same store. The entire loop closes in under 15 minutes — a returns experience that no e-commerce player can match.

This turns the dark store network from a grocery-delivery cost center into a **high-margin fulfillment advantage** that justifies the ₹2.5 crore capex per store.

## Page 06/08 — Pillar 03: Operational Trust — The Infrastructure Behind the Promise

Speed without reliability is a marketing claim. Operational trust — in forecasting, rider welfare, and proactive recovery — is what turns a 10-minute promise into a habit.

### Initiative 1: AI Demand Sensing

A dark store running 8,000+ SKUs across grocery, electronics, and beauty cannot forecast demand with static reorder points. The proposal is a **store-level ML forecasting layer** that ingests local weather, event calendars, neighborhood purchase cadence, and macro signals (IPL match days, festival weeks) to generate rolling 72-hour demand curves per SKU per store. The goal: cut stockout-driven cancellations by 30% within three quarters while simultaneously reducing excess inventory write-offs — the two failure modes that eat contribution margin from opposite sides.

### Initiative 2: Rider Operations Intelligence

Blinkit's rider network is a **gig workforce exceeding 200,000** across 150+ cities. Rider churn directly impacts delivery speed: every new rider needs 2–3 weeks to hit peak routing efficiency. A **Rider Welfare Dashboard** — real-time fatigue scoring, heat-stress alerts, earnings-path transparency — isn't just an ESG initiative. It's a retention play: reducing churn by even 10% saves weeks of onboarding friction, keeps experienced riders on the road, and directly protects the SLA.

### Initiative 3: Proactive Customer Recovery

Today, the support flow is reactive — a customer complains, then the system responds. A **Proactive Support Engine** monitors live signals: if a delivery is trending 5+ minutes late, if a substitution was made, or if a high-value item was flagged as damaged at the store — and triggers a resolution (credit, callback, or re-dispatch) *before* the customer contacts support. The target is a 40% reduction in inbound support tickets for delivery-related complaints.

> **Why this pillar last — but not least:** Pillars 1 and 2 generate revenue and reach. Pillar 3 protects the unit economics that make both sustainable. Without demand sensing, stores overstock. Without rider welfare, churn eats the SLA. Without proactive support, NPS erodes — and with it, repeat rates.

## Page 08/08 — Conclusion: Why Blinkit Wins — And What It Takes to Keep Winning

Three pillars, nine initiatives, one thesis: the company that controls the full loop — demand forecasting, inventory, last-mile, and post-purchase trust — owns the category. No hybrid compromises, no speed sacrifices.

### Executive Summary

Blinkit enters FY27 as India's largest quick-commerce platform by every meaningful metric — market share, dark-store count, and adjusted-EBITDA trajectory. But leadership in a category growing 60–80% annually is perishable. The playbook that delivered dominance — sprint-speed expansion, deep metro density, and a 10-minute brand promise — is now table stakes. Every competitor has the same playbook.

This roadmap argues that **the next phase of competitive advantage comes from three places**: extracting durable margin from ad-tech without degrading the shopper experience (Pillar 1), making Tier-2/3 expansion self-funding through MOV optimization, high-margin assortment, and dark-store-powered reverse logistics (Pillar 2), and hardening the operational substrate — demand sensing, rider welfare, proactive recovery — that makes the speed promise credible at 3,000+ stores (Pillar 3).

Together, these nine initiatives don't just grow revenue — they change the cost structure. Predictive Bidding and Sponsored Sampling turn existing traffic into high-margin ad revenue at near-zero incremental cost. MOV Tiers lift Tier-2/3 basket sizes while keeping the 10-minute delivery promise intact — protecting Blinkit's core moat. High-Margin Assortment with 10-minute reverse logistics unlocks categories (electronics, beauty, apparel) that structurally improve contribution margin per order. AI Demand Sensing attacks the hidden margin killer — stockout-driven cancellations that cost both the sale and the customer's trust in selection reliability.

### Strategic Recommendations

1. **Ship MOV Tiers and Predictive Bidding in Q3 FY27** — highest RICE scores, near-zero capex, immediate contribution-margin lift. Every quarter of delay is revenue left on the table.

2. **Launch Sponsored Sampling with 10 anchor brands** — convert offline trial budgets into a measurable digital format. Target: 5% sample-to-purchase conversion within 30 days.

3. **Build Demand Sensing on the existing data stack** — this is an ML model, not a new platform. Start with the top 200 SKUs per store and expand by level.

4. **Launch the Rider Welfare Dashboard alongside** MOV Tiers — the same cities optimizing basket economics need the strongest rider retention.

5. **Gate every Tier-2/3 store with the Level System** — no store gets L3+ inventory (including high-margin electronics, beauty, apparel) without proving it can maintain a 90% fill rate and positive CM at L2.

### Risk Register

| Risk | Severity | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|----------|------------|------------|
| **Ad load degrades UX** — higher CPMs drive placement bloat | High | Medium | Hard-cap ad density per session; A/B test every placement change against conversion rate, not just revenue. |
| **Tier-2/3 AOV stays below breakeven** — MOV threshold deters price-sensitive users | High | Medium | Dynamic MOV calibrated weekly per city; multi-pack nudges and private-label placement to make threshold feel like a deal, not a tax. Auto-pause free delivery in stores below CM target for 4 consecutive weeks. |
| **Returns abuse in high-margin categories** — serial returners erode margin | Medium | Medium | Return-rate scoring per customer; cap free returns at 3/month; require photo verification before rider dispatch. |
| **Rider welfare regulation tightens** — gig-worker legislation in key states | Medium | Medium | Proactive compliance via the Rider Welfare Dashboard — if Blinkit sets the standard, regulation follows Blinkit's model. |
| **Demand Sensing model underperforms** — cold-start problem in new stores | Medium | Low | Start with top 200 SKUs per store; fall back to rule-based reorder for long-tail. Expand model coverage only after validation. |

### The Thesis, Restated

> The 10-minute promise got Blinkit to leadership. Defending that position requires a fundamentally different capability set: monetization precision that scales revenue without scaling costs, a reach model that adapts to India beyond the metros, and an operational trust layer that makes reliability — not just speed — the brand promise customers remember. The company that masters all three doesn't just lead the category. It *becomes* the category.
